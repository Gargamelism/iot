## Goal

Give each voice an arbitrary harmonic recipe that stays alias-free across the whole pitch
range while costing about one table lookup per sample.

## You'll be able to

- Build a periodic waveform from a list of harmonic amplitudes (a Fourier series), and
  predict the spectrum of saw, square, triangle and "octaves only".
- Explain **aliasing**: why a naïve sawtooth at 1 kHz is full of inharmonic junk at 44.1 kHz,
  and what "band-limited" means.
- Design a **mip-mapped** set of wavetables: how many harmonics each pitch range may keep,
  and what that costs in RAM.
- Normalize tables so every timbre peaks at the same level without ever exceeding the
  mixer's bound.
- Reason about the trade-offs: table size, interpolation, band-limiting edge, and number of
  tables.

## Concepts

- **Timbre as a spectrum.** Harmonic *n* sits at *n·f₀*. A "spread" is the function
  *n → amplitude* (for example 1/n, odd-only, 1/n², octave-only).
- **Fourier series** of the classic waves, and the **Gibbs** overshoot.
- **Additive synthesis**: computing the sum per sample versus **precomputing one period**
  into a table. The harmonic sum is a fixed function of phase, so it can be baked.
- **Aliasing / foldover.** A component above Nyquist (22.05 kHz) reflects to `fs − f`. A
  harmonic at 23 kHz plays as 21.1 kHz, and as pitch rises it *falls*.
- **Band-limiting by table.** Keep only harmonics `n ≤ (fs/2) / f₀`. Because f₀ varies, you
  need several tables (a **mip-map**, typically one per octave) and pick one per note.
- **Which edge of the range to band-limit against.** Limiting against the bottom of each
  range keeps brightness but aliases near the top. Limiting against the top is always safe
  but slightly dull just above each boundary.
- **Peak vs worst case.** Normalizing weights to sum to 1 bounds the worst-case peak (all
  harmonics in phase). Real sums peak much lower, so you can rescale to the *measured* peak
  to use the full range.
- **Memory arithmetic.** RAM = spreads × levels × table length × 2 bytes. Only build what a
  voice can actually reach.

## Explore (notebook)

`07-wavetables.ipynb`:

1. **Build** one period of saw, square and triangle from their harmonic weights (sum of
   sines) and plot them over the "ideal" shapes. Find the Gibbs overshoot.
2. **Alias on purpose.** Generate a *naïve* sawtooth (a raw phase ramp) at 1,000 Hz and
   3,520 Hz, fs = 44.1 kHz, then plot the log spectrum. Mark the true harmonics and label
   the folded ones.
3. **Fix it.** Compare a band-limited version that keeps `n ≤ 22050 / f₀`. Listen to both
   (`sounddevice`).
4. **Mip-map design.**
   - For an 80 Hz–1 kHz instrument with headroom for chords and vibrato above it, tabulate,
     per octave, how many harmonics you can keep when limiting against the octave's top edge
     versus its bottom edge.
   - Plot the spectrum of a note just *below* a boundary against one just *above* it. That is
     the timbre jump you'll listen for on hardware.
5. **Normalization.** For each spread, compare (a) Σ weights and (b) the actual peak of the
   built table. Which spreads waste the most headroom under (a)? Then randomize the harmonic
   phases: the peak changes, but does the sound?
6. **RAM.** Write a function `bytes(spreads, levels, table_len)` and use it to fit your
   design under 128 KB. How much do you save by skipping levels no voice can reach?
7. **Verify the real thing** (if you have an audio interface): record your device sweeping
   the saw, compute a spectrogram, and look for diagonal lines moving against the pitch.
   Those are aliases.

**Aha:** the expensive part of additive synthesis can move to boot time, and band-limiting
becomes a question of *which table*.

## Build

Start from your module 06 DDS oscillator.

**Add:**

- **Spectra as data.** A set of named spectra, each a function or table giving harmonic *n*'s
  amplitude: at least sine, saw, square, triangle and one of your own. Adding a new spectrum
  must not touch the audio path. *Examples* (with *n* = 1 as the fundamental):
  - saw: `1/n`
  - square: `1/n` for odd *n*, 0 for even
  - triangle: `1/n²` for odd *n*, 0 for even (alternating sign)
  - "octaves": `1/n` only when *n* is a power of two
  - your own, for example a hollow spectrum `{1, 0, 0.5, 0, 0.3, 0.6, 0, 0.2}`

  ```cpp
  // illustrative only: a spectrum is just "harmonic number -> amplitude"
  float sawWeight(int n)    { return 1.0f / n; }
  float squareWeight(int n) { return (n % 2) ? 1.0f / n : 0.0f; }
  ```
- **Runtime switching** between them without reflashing. Use the encoder button temporarily,
  or a Serial command. *Example:* typing `s 2` in the Serial Monitor switches to spectrum 2
  and prints `spectrum: square`.
- **No audible aliasing** across the full pitch range, with headroom up to at least an octave
  above the pot's maximum (later modules play chords and vibrato above the root). *Example:*
  at 2 kHz a saw may keep only harmonics up to n = 11 (22,050 / 2,000). At 80 Hz it could keep
  275, far more than you need.
- **Equal peak level** for every spectrum. No spectrum may ever exceed the per-voice peak the
  mixer will assume in module 08. *Example:* if you pick a per-voice peak of ±32,000, every
  table's largest sample is 32,000 or just under it, whether it holds a sine or a 64-harmonic
  saw.
- **A boot report.** *Example:*

  ```
  tables: 5 spectra x 6 levels x 2048 B = 61440 B (budget 131072), free heap 182344
  osc: 13.2 cycles/voice/sample (256-frame block)
  ```

**Constraints:**

- ≤ 20 cycles per voice per sample on the audio path. Measure it.
- Total table RAM within a budget you state up front (≤ 128 KB in the reference).
- Any heavy computation happens at boot, and boot stays under about 1 s.
- The audio contract applies. Choosing a table when the frequency changes happens at block
  rate, not per sample.

**Deliverable:** your oscillator reading band-limited tables, a boot log of the table memory,
and a written note of your band-limiting choices.

## Design questions

1. Precompute or sum at runtime? Count the cycles of a runtime harmonic loop on the C3 (no
   FPU, so integer sine lookups). How many harmonics could one voice afford, and how many
   voices does that leave?
2. How many harmonics does your lowest note need before more stops being audible? (Hint: 80
   Hz × n against your hearing and your speaker's range.)
3. Band-limit against the top or the bottom of each range? One table per octave, or per
   half-octave? Quantify the dullness against the RAM.
4. Should you crossfade between adjacent tables to hide the boundary jump? What does that
   cost per sample?
5. Table length and interpolation: 1,024 with no interpolation, or 256 with linear? Use the
   EarLevel SNR article and your cycle budget.
6. Will any future spectrum depend on *absolute* Hz rather than harmonic number (think body
   resonances, module 15)? If so, at what frequency do you evaluate its weights for a given
   table?

## Compare (spoiler)

One answer lives in `synth/oscillator.h`, `synth/oscillator.cpp` and
`synth/harmonic_spreads.h`.

- `harmonic_spreads.h`: each spectrum ("spread") is a weight function. Everything funnels
  through `harmonicWeightTerm(spread, h, harmonicHz)`, which receives the harmonic's *Hz*
  so the instrument models in module 15 can place resonances.
- `initWavetables()` builds only spreads some patch uses, and only up to
  `maxMipLevelForSpread()`, the highest level any voice can reach plus margins for scale
  snapping and vibrato. `wavetableBytesAllocated()` is printed at boot against
  `WAVETABLE_RAM_BUDGET_BYTES`.
- `harmonicsForMipLevel()` band-limits against the **top** of each octave.
  `mipLevelCentreHz()` is where Hz-dependent weights are evaluated.
- `buildTable()` normalizes the weights to sum to `VOLUME_Q15_ONE` (the worst-case bound),
  then **rescales to the measured peak** so every table uses the full `SINE_TABLE_AMPLITUDE`.
  Compare this with your question about headroom.
- `Oscillator::setFrequency()` picks the mip level once per block. `nextSample()` is a single
  lookup with **no interpolation** into 1,024-entry tables.
- `plans/2026-09-06_max_voices_and_distortion_headroom.md` has the measured numbers behind
  "precompute": about 14 cycles per harmonic at runtime against about 13 cycles per voice
  with tables.
