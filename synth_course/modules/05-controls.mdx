## Goal

Turn noisy, bouncy physical inputs into clean, musically mapped parameters and hand them
safely to the audio task.

## You'll be able to

- Explain where ADC jitter comes from and trade **noise against latency** knowingly, with
  oversampling, an exponential moving average and hysteresis.
- Map a knob to pitch and volume the way the ear hears them, not the way the voltage moves.
- Decode a quadrature encoder reliably with a state table, and say why it rejects bounce
  for free.
- Debounce a push button and read a switch with an internal pull-up.
- Publish a group of related parameters from `loop()` to the audio task without the audio
  side ever waiting.

## Concepts

- **The pot as a voltage divider.** The ESP32-C3's 12-bit SAR ADC, its attenuation settings
  (usable input range), and its nonlinearity near the rails.
- **Noise reduction.** Averaging N samples cuts white noise by √N. The **EMA**
  `y += α(x − y)` is a one-pole low-pass, and its time constant depends on *how often you call
  it*. A **hysteresis/deadband** stops a value sitting on a code boundary from flickering.
- **Perceptual mapping.** Pitch is heard logarithmically, so equal rotation should give an
  equal frequency *ratio*: `f = fmin · (fmax/fmin)^x`. Loudness also works in ratios (dB).
  Compare a linear and an "audio taper" volume.
- **Resolution at the low end.** Work out how many cents one ADC count is worth at 80 Hz
  versus at 1 kHz under an exponential map.
- **Quadrature encoders.** The 2-bit Gray code (00→01→11→10). A 16-entry transition table
  gives +1/−1/0, and impossible jumps score 0, so contact bounce cancels itself. The
  *detent* does not equal the *edge*: this EC11 clicks every 2 edges.
- **Interrupts on a single core.** An ISR preempts even the max-priority audio task. Keep it
  tiny and in IRAM, share a counter with a critical section, and drain it from `loop()`.
- **Debouncing.** Mechanical bounce lasts a few ms, so lock out further edges for a fixed
  time after one.
- **Control rate vs audio rate.** `loop()` runs only when the audio task yields, about once
  per audio block. Parameters change at that rate, which hints at zipper noise (module 11).

## Explore (notebook)

`05-controls.ipynb`:

1. **Real data first.** Log 2,000 raw ADC readings of a *stationary* pot over Serial, paste
   them in, and plot a histogram. How many codes wide is the noise? Is it Gaussian?
2. **A simulated sweep.** Make a slow ramp plus a sudden jump of a "true" pot value, then add
   your measured noise and 12-bit quantization.
3. Run it through (a) raw, (b) oversampled N = 1/4/8/16, (c) EMA at several α, (d) EMA plus
   hysteresis at several thresholds.
4. For each, plot the **residual jitter** (peak-to-peak while stationary) against the
   **latency** (time to 90% after the jump). Pick your operating point and say why.
5. **Mapping.** Plot Hz against pot position for linear and exponential maps. Then plot
   *cents per ADC count* along the travel. Where is the exponential map coarsest?
6. **Hysteresis vs resolution.** With your chosen threshold, what is the smallest pitch step
   the pot can make at 80 Hz? Is it audible? (A just-noticeable difference is roughly 5–10
   cents.)

**Aha:** smoothing is a filter, so it always costs latency, and the right amount depends on
how often `loop()` runs.

## Play (playground)

**Knob lab.**
- A virtual knob drives a sine through a *simulated* noisy 12-bit ADC.
- Toggles for oversampling (N), EMA (α slider) and hysteresis (threshold slider), plus a
  linear/exponential mapping switch.
- Live readouts: Hz, note name, and a scrolling trace of the parameter.
- You should *hear* pitch jitter vanish as smoothing goes up, and *feel* the knob get sluggish
  when α gets too small.

**Encoder lab.**
- Shows A/B waveforms for a turn, with a "bounce" slider that injects random contact chatter.
- Two decoders run side by side: a naive "count A's rising edges, read B" decoder and the
  16-entry transition table, each with its step counter.
- You should see the naive decoder miscount as bounce goes up while the table stays exact.

**Reuses** from `web_sim/app.js`: `smoothValue`, `mapPitchHz`, `adcNoise`, `freqToNoteName`
and the AudioWorklet sine. **New:** the encoder waveform renderer, the bounce injector and
the naive decoder.

## Build

Start from your module 04 firmware: an audio task playing a fixed sine, with the liveness LED
in `loop()`.

**Add:**

- **Pitch pot** → the sine's frequency, 80 Hz to 1 kHz, equal rotation for equal musical
  interval. *Example:* the pot at ¼, ½ and ¾ of its travel should read about 150 Hz (D3),
  280 Hz (C#4) and 530 Hz (C5). Each quarter is the same ratio, about 1.88×.
- **Volume pot** → master level, from true silence to full scale. *Example:* fully
  counter-clockwise gives a sample peak of exactly 0, not "very quiet". Fully clockwise peaks
  at the level you chose in module 03.
- **Gate switch** (SPST to GND) → mute/unmute. *Example:* flip it ten times quickly and get
  ten clean mutes and unmutes, with no stuck state.
- **Encoder rotation** → a signed step counter, exactly one step per detent. Print it on
  change. *Example:* three clicks clockwise print `enc +1`, `enc +2`, `enc +3`; one click back
  prints `enc +2`. Module 10 turns this into patch selection.
- **Encoder button** → a boolean toggle. Print it on change. *Example:* `btn on`, `btn off`,
  one line per press.
- A single `Serial` debug line that is printed only when something changed. *Example* (the
  format is up to you):

  ```
  vol 0.62  pitch 0.494 -> 278.4 Hz C#4  gate 1  enc +3  btn on
  ```

*Example API shape* (illustrative only; yours can differ):

```cpp
void controlsBegin();   // pin modes, interrupts, initial reads
void controlsUpdate();  // read, smooth, map, publish; called from loop()
// The audio task reads a snapshot of the published parameters once per block,
// for example: struct Params { float freqHz; float volume; bool gate; ... };
```

**Constraints:**

- The audio contract (see the curriculum): the audio task must never wait on the controls. If
  fresh parameters aren't available this block, it reuses last block's.
- The parameters the audio task reads must be *mutually consistent*. You will soon publish
  several values that must never be seen half-updated (for example a patch index with its
  frequencies).
- The ISR (if you use one) does no `Serial`, no allocation, no floats, and must be
  IRAM-resident.
- Pins are fixed by the board. Check `pins.h` for which GPIOs are actually free, or work it
  out from the board's pinout yourself.

**Deliverable:** a `controls` unit with a begin/update API called from `setup()`/`loop()`,
and the parameter handoff to the audio task.

## Design questions

Answer these in your notes before you open Compare.

1. Does your smoothing run in `loop()` or in the audio task? What is `loop()`'s actual call
   rate, and is it steady? Measure it. What happens to your EMA's time constant when the
   display refresh (module 10) makes some `loop()` iterations slower?
2. Rotation: interrupt or polling? Use the fastest spin you can do by hand and the measured
   `loop()` period to justify your choice. Is the button different?
3. What exactly is the race between your ISR and the code that reads its counter? What is the
   smallest critical section that closes it?
4. Should the hysteresis compare the new reading against the *filtered* value or against the
   *last accepted raw* reading? What failure does each choice have?
5. Volume: linear amplitude or dB? Try both and describe how the bottom third of the knob
   feels.
6. A gate edge is an *event*, but the audio task samples your parameters only once per
   block. How do you make sure it can't miss a quick on-off-on? (Think: a flag versus a
   counter.)

## Compare (spoiler)

One answer lives in `synth/controls.cpp`, `synth/controls_math.cpp` and the `ADC_*` and
`ENCODER_*` constants in `synth/pins.h`.

- `readOversampled()` averages `ADC_OVERSAMPLE_COUNT` = 8 reads. Then `smoothValue()` applies
  an EMA (`ADC_EMA_ALPHA` 0.15) gated by hysteresis (`ADC_HYSTERESIS_COUNTS` 4). Notice the
  gate compares against the **last accepted raw** reading, not the filtered value.
- `mapPitchHz()` is the exponential map. Volume is mapped **linearly**: is that what you
  chose?
- `encoderISR()` is `IRAM_ATTR`, reads both pins and accumulates `quadratureStep()`. The
  16-entry table scores bounce as 0. `updateEncoderRotation()` drains it under a `portMUX`
  critical section and converts edges to detents (`ENCODER_STEPS_PER_DETENT` = 2). The
  comment says why rotation needs the ISR and the button doesn't.
- `updateEncoderButton()`: time-lockout debounce (`ENCODER_BUTTON_DEBOUNCE_MS`).
- `controlsUpdate()` publishes everything in **one** locked block into `g_oscParams`.
  `controls.h` explains why the fields must move together. The audio side
  (`audio_task.cpp`) only ever uses a zero-timeout `xSemaphoreTake`.
- `OscParams::retriggerSeq` is a **counter**, not an edge flag. That is the answer to
  question 6.
