## Goal

Make sustained notes feel *played*: a low-frequency oscillator that adds vibrato (pitch) and
tremolo (amplitude), fading in after the note starts.

## You'll be able to

- Build an LFO and apply it to pitch (in cents) and to amplitude.
- Explain why a delayed, fading-in vibrato sounds human and a constant one sounds mechanical.
- Reason about modulation at block rate: is 172 Hz update rate enough for a 6 Hz LFO?
- Keep modulation from pushing voices past their band-limited range.

## Concepts

- **LFO.** A sine (or other shape) at 3–8 Hz, its phase advanced per block.
- **Vibrato.** Frequency × `2^(depth·lfo/1200)`. Players use roughly 5–7 Hz and ±10–50 cents.
- **Tremolo.** Gain × `(1 − depth·(0.5 + 0.5·lfo))`. Flutes have a lot of it.
- **Onset shaping.** Hold the depth at 0 for a delay, then ramp it in over an onset time.
  Restart both on every note.
- **Sidebands.** FM at audio rates creates sidebands. At LFO rates it's just a slow pitch
  wobble, but it still needs headroom in your mip-map (module 07).
- **Block-rate modulation.** Updating the frequency every 5.8 ms stair-steps the pitch. Can
  you hear it at 6 Hz?

## Explore (notebook)

`13-modulation.ipynb`:

1. Render 2 s of a 330 Hz saw with vibrato at 5.5 Hz ±25 cents: plain, with a 300 ms delay,
   and with delay plus a 200 ms onset. Listen to all three.
2. Track the pitch of each (zero crossings or STFT peak) and plot it against time.
3. **Block-rate staircase.** Recompute the same vibrato with the frequency updated every 256
   samples. Overlay its pitch track and listen for a difference.
4. **Headroom.** Compute the highest frequency a voice reaches at max pot + max offset + max
   vibrato depth, and check it against your mip-map's top level.

## Build

Start from module 12.

**Add:**

- **One LFO per patch**, configured from the table, restarted on each note. *Example* config
  (illustrative):

  ```cpp
  struct Vib { float rateHz; uint8_t depthCents; uint16_t delayMs, onsetMs; uint8_t tremoloPct; };
  constexpr Vib kFlute = {5.5f, 25, 250, 200, 15};
  constexpr Vib kNone  = {0, 0, 0, 0, 0};
  ```
- **Pitch modulation** as a multiplier applied to every voice's frequency at block rate.
  *Example:* ±25 cents is a ratio from 0.98567 to 1.01454.
- **Amplitude modulation**, multiplying the envelope's block gain (so the per-sample ramp
  smooths it too).
- **A debug line.** *Example:* `vib: onset 0.63, pitch x1.0091, amp x0.94`.

**Constraints:**

- The LFO runs at block rate. Floats are allowed there.
- Nothing new on the per-sample path. Reuse the existing gain ramp and the frequency set.
- Your mip-map headroom must cover the deepest vibrato on the highest note.

**Deliverable:** an LFO unit, per-patch vibrato configs, and wiring into pitch and gain.

## Design questions

1. Should vibrato phase reset on every note? What does a random starting phase sound like?
2. Does a patch switch mid-note restart the vibrato?
3. Should vibrato depth scale with volume (players often add vibrato on louder, longer
   notes)?
4. You apply vibrato to *every* voice in the same phase. What would per-voice phase offsets
   do to a chord?

## Compare (spoiler)

- `synth/vibrato.h` and `vibrato.cpp`: `VibratoConfig` holds `{rateHz, depthCents, delayMs,
  onsetMs, tremoloPercent}`. `advanceBlock()` computes the onset fade, then
  `pitchMultiplier_` (via `exp2f`) and `amplitudeMultiplier_`. `noteOn()` resets the phase
  and onset, and `configure()` deliberately doesn't (question 2).
- `synth/audio_task.cpp`: `s_vibrato.advanceBlock()` runs once per block, its amplitude
  multiplier scales `targetGainQ15`, and `pitchMultiplier` is applied in each voice's
  `setFrequency()`.
- `synth/oscillator.cpp` `maxMipLevelForSpread()`: `kDetuneMarginCents` and
  `kDiatonicMarginSemitones` cover vibrato and snapping.
- `synth/voices.h`: `kVibratoReed`, `kVibratoFlute`, `kVibratoBrass`, `kVibratoBowed`,
  `kVibratoPlucked` and the deliberately extreme `kVibratoAlien`.
