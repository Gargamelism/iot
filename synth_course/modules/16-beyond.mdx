## Goal

Go past the reference synth with one self-directed project, using everything from modules
00–15.

## You'll be able to

- Scope, budget and validate a DSP feature on your own, with no reference answer.
- Read primary sources (papers, the CCRMA books) and turn them into fixed-point firmware.

## Concepts

Each project has its own:

- **Random harmonic phases.** Lower crest factor means more headroom and a less "buzzy"
  sum of detuned voices.
- **Room / reverb.** Comb and all-pass networks (Schroeder, Freeverb), and delay memory
  against the RAM budget.
- **Karplus-Strong.** A delay line plus a lowpass in a feedback loop: a physical model of a
  plucked string with real decay and inharmonic detail that tables can't give you.
- **Per-voice envelopes.** Each chord note has its own life, as in a real piano.
- **External control (MIDI).** The C3's native USB is a serial/JTAG port, not a USB-MIDI
  device, and the board has no GPIO left. Bridging serial to MIDI on the host is the
  practical path.

## Read

Pick the readings for your project.

- **Random phases:** `plans/2026-09-11_realistic_instrument_dynamics.md`, *Phase 6*;
  revisit the module 01 phase experiment.
- **Reverb:** J. O. Smith III, *Physical Audio Signal Processing*, **["Artificial Reverberation"](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~jos/pasp/Artificial_Reverberation.html)**,
  then the **["Schroeder Reverberators"](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~jos/pasp/Schroeder_Reverberators.html)** and **["Freeverb"](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~jos/pasp/Freeverb.html)** pages
  (≈3 h).
- **Karplus-Strong:**
  - K. Karplus & A. Strong, "Digital Synthesis of Plucked-String and Drum Timbres",
    *Computer Music Journal* 7(2):43–55, 1983, via libraries or JSTOR; Karplus's own
    [notes on the algorithm](https://users.soe.ucsc.edu/~karplus/digitar.html) are free (≈1.5 h)
  - J. O. Smith III, *PASP*, **["The Karplus-Strong Algorithm"](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~jos/pasp/Karplus_Strong_Algorithm.html)** (≈1 h)
  - Pirkle, *[Designing Software Synthesizer Plugins in C++](https://www.routledge.com/Designing-Software-Synthesizer-Plugins-in-C-With-Audio-DSP/Pirkle/p/book/9780367510466)* 2e, **ch 13 Karplus-Strong
    Plucked String Model** (≈2 h)
- **Per-voice envelopes:** Pirkle **ch 3 Synth Voices, Synth Modules, and Module Cores**
  (≈1.5 h).
- **MIDI over serial:** the [*hairless-midiserial*](https://projectgus.github.io/hairless-midiserial/) bridge, plus the
  MIDI 1.0 message format (note on/off, CC) from any MIDI primer (≈1 h).

## Explore (notebook)

Write `16-beyond.ipynb` yourself. Start with a float prototype of your feature in numpy,
render audio, and only then port it. *Examples:*
- **Reverb:** the impulse response and decay (RT60) of your comb/all-pass network.
- **Karplus-Strong:** the decay time against pitch, and the effect of the loop filter.
- **Random phases:** the peak against RMS for 32 detuned voices, with aligned versus random
  phases.

## Build

Pick **one**.

**Add (examples of acceptance criteria):**

- **Random harmonic phases.** *Example:* the boot log shows each table's peak/RMS dropping by
  at least 2 dB, the 9-voice "PULS" patch sounds no worse, and you can raise its level by the
  gained headroom.
- **Room.** *Example:* a Schroeder reverb (4 combs + 2 all-pass) on the mix at 22 kHz, with a
  wet/dry amount per patch. RAM for delay lines ≤ 32 KB, total load still ≤ 60%. *Example*
  log line: `reverb: 4 combs (1116..1617 samp), 2 AP, 27.6 KB, +410 cyc/frame`.
- **Karplus-Strong voice.** *Example:* a new "KS" spectrum type whose voices use a delay line
  instead of a table. A note at 110 Hz decays over ~3 s, pitch accuracy is within ±5 cents
  (fractional delay), and you reach 8 voices within budget.
- **Per-voice envelopes.** *Example:* in a 3-voice piano chord, the top note decays twice as
  fast as the bottom one. Report the load difference against a single shared envelope.
- **MIDI in over USB serial.** *Example:* a host bridge sends note-on/off. The synth plays the
  received note as the root (overriding the pitch pot), and note-off releases.

**Constraints:**

- The audio contract, as always.
- State your RAM and cycle budget before you start, and report the measured numbers at the
  end.

**Deliverable:** the feature, your acceptance criteria, and the measured budget.

## Design questions

Write your own. At minimum:

1. What does this cost per sample, per block and in RAM, and where exactly does it sit in the
   chain?
2. What's the simplest version that would convince you it works?
3. What's the failure mode if it misbehaves on the audio task, and how do you bound it?
