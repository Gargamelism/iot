## Goal

Make the voices play *music*: a pitch pot that snaps to a key, and chords whose quality follows
the root's position in the scale, all computed rather than stored.

## You'll be able to

- Convert between Hz, MIDI note numbers and cents, in 12-tone equal temperament.
- Represent scales and modes as interval patterns and index them by scale degree, including
  negative degrees and degrees beyond the octave.
- Explain why stacking scale *degrees* (not semitones) gives diatonic chord qualities for
  free.
- Snap a continuous control to the nearest in-key note.

## Concepts

- **12-TET.** `f = 440 · 2^((m − 69)/12)`. Why equal temperament is a compromise: compare just
  intervals (5:4, 3:2) with their 12-TET versions in cents.
- **Scales as patterns.** Major `{0,2,4,5,7,9,11}`, natural minor, harmonic minor, melodic
  minor, Phrygian, minor pentatonic (5 notes!) and chromatic.
- **Scale degrees.** Index with floor division and modulo so that degree −1 is the note below
  the root, not a wrap to the top.
- **Diatonic harmony.** Stacking degrees 0/2/4 on each root gives the triad qualities
  (I ii iii IV V vi vii°). In code, the chord quality is *derived*, never stored.
- **Snapping.** Map continuous MIDI onto the nearest degree in the mode, with care at the
  boundaries (hysteresis from module 05 helps).
- **Events from continuous input.** A "new note" is when the snapped degree *changes*.

## Explore (notebook)

`09-scales.ipynb`:

1. **Tuning table.** For unison through octave, list the just ratio, the 12-TET ratio and the
   difference in cents. Which interval is worst?
2. **Beats of tempered thirds.** At A3, compute the beat rate between a 12-TET major third's
   5th harmonic and the root's 4th harmonic. That beating is part of what a "piano" sounds
   like.
3. **Scale-degree math.** Implement `degree_to_semitone(degree, mode)` with floor-div/mod and
   test it on degrees −8…+15 in every mode.
4. **Chord quality table.** For each mode, stack 0/2/4 on every degree and classify each
   triad (major, minor, diminished, augmented) by its semitone pattern.
5. **Snap.** Implement `snap(midi, mode)` and plot the pot position against the snapped
   degree. Then add hysteresis and show the boundary stops flickering on noisy input.

## Play (playground)

**Key explorer.**
- Pick a key root and a mode. A virtual pitch knob sweeps and snaps.
- Shows the current root, the chord notes, the chord quality and a piano-keyboard view.
- A voice-offset editor sets the degree for each voice.
- You should hear the chord *quality* change as the root moves, with no table of chords
  anywhere.

**Reuses** `freqToNoteName` and `mapPitchHz` from `web_sim/app.js`, and the module 08
mixer. **New:** the scale engine and the keyboard view.

## Build

Start from module 08 (N voices at fixed semitone offsets).

**Add:**

- **A key and mode.** A compile-time key root and a set of modes. *Example:* key root E2
  (MIDI 40), modes `MAJOR, MINOR, HARMONIC_MINOR, PHRYGIAN, PENTATONIC_MINOR, CHROMATIC`.
- **A snapped root.** The pitch pot snaps to the nearest scale degree. *Example* in E natural
  minor: a pot position that maps to 180 Hz (MIDI 53.5, between E3 and F#3) snaps to F#3
  (185.0 Hz), degree 8.
- **Voices offset in degrees.** *Examples:*
  - In E natural minor, degrees 0/2/4 from E give E-G-B (minor), and from G give G-B-D
    (major).
  - In E harmonic minor (raised 7th, D#), 0/2/4 from B gives B-D#-F#: the major V chord that
    natural minor lacks.
- **Negative and wide degrees.** *Example:* degree −1 from E in E minor is D (one step down,
  previous octave); degree 7 is E an octave up.
- **A note-change event.** *Example:* `root F#3 (deg 8) -> chord F#3 A3 C4 (dim)`.

**Constraints:**

- The scale math runs in `loop()`, not in the audio task. The audio task receives
  frequencies.
- Recompute frequencies only when the snapped root or the voice set changes.
- The scale math is host-testable: no Arduino headers in it.

**Deliverable:** a scale unit (degree ↔ semitone ↔ Hz, plus snapping) and voices driven by it.

## Design questions

1. A pentatonic scale has 5 degrees per octave, chromatic has 12. How does your code handle
   "degrees per octave" varying by mode?
2. Snapping: nearest semitone first and then nearest degree, or nearest degree directly?
   What's the cheapest correct search?
3. When the patch's pitch mode is "detuned unison", should the scale code run at all? How do
   you make that obvious in the data?
4. A new root is an event. Should moving the pot while the gate is *open* (silent) still
   register it?

## Compare (spoiler)

- `synth/scale.h` and `scale.cpp`:
  - `kScales` holds the interval patterns, with a per-mode `degreesPerOctave`, and
    `floorDiv()` makes negative degrees walk downward.
  - `scaleDegreeToSemitone()`, then `scaleDegreeToFreq()` via `notes.h` `midiToFreq()`.
  - `snapMidiToScaleDegree()` searches only the degree either side of a rounded guess.
  - `SCALE_KEY_ROOT_MIDI` is 40 (E2).
  - `SCALE_NOT_WORKING` is how the table says "this patch ignores scales" (question 3).
- `synth/controls.cpp` `updateVoiceFreqs()`, `PITCH_DIATONIC` branch: it recomputes only on a
  new root, and the comment explains why offsets are in *degrees*. `s_retriggerSeq++` is the
  note event, skipped for the boot or patch-switch sentinel.
- `synth/test/test_scale.cpp`: the repo's host tests for this math.
