## Goal

Add deliberate nonlinearity, in several flavors, safely and cheaply, and understand what it
does to a spectrum (including to a chord).

## You'll be able to

- Predict which harmonics a waveshaper adds (odd vs even, how fast they fall).
- Implement clipping, soft saturation, wavefolding and bit reduction in integer math, with
  bounded cost.
- Explain intermodulation and choose where in the chain to distort.
- Recognize aliasing caused by nonlinearity, and decide whether it matters.

## Concepts

- **Waveshaping.** `y = f(x)` applied per sample. Its *transfer curve* determines the spectrum
  it adds.
- **Symmetry.** An odd-symmetric `f` (`f(−x) = −f(x)`) adds only odd harmonics. An asymmetric
  `f` adds even harmonics too.
- **Hard clip** (gain then clamp): many high harmonics, harsh.
- **Soft clip.** A smooth curve such as cubic `x − x³/3` adds mostly the 3rd harmonic, so it
  sounds warm.
- **Foldback.** Reflect the signal at a threshold instead of clamping: metallic, rich and
  pitch-dependent. Bound the loop for real-time safety.
- **Bitcrush.** Zero the low bits to give quantization as an effect: noisy and lo-fi.
- **Drive.** Gain before the shaper sets *how much* nonlinearity you get.
- **Intermodulation.** Distorting a *sum* creates sum and difference tones between voices.
  Distorting each voice separately doesn't.
- **Nonlinearity aliases.** New harmonics above Nyquist fold back. Oversampling is the
  textbook cure, and usually unaffordable here.
- **No divides on the hot path.** The C3's hardware divide takes many cycles. Use a
  multiply by a reciprocal instead.

## Explore (notebook)

`14-distortion.ipynb`:

1. Plot the transfer curves of hard clip, cubic soft clip, foldback and bitcrush at drive
   1, 3 and 6.
2. Feed a 220 Hz sine through each and plot the spectra. Confirm odd-only harmonics for the
   symmetric shapers.
3. **Asymmetry.** Add a DC offset before a soft clip and watch even harmonics appear.
4. **Intermodulation.** Run an A-major triad of sines through a hard clip (a) after summing
   and (b) per voice and then summed. Mark the sum and difference products.
5. **Aliasing.** Hard-clip a 2 kHz sine at drive 6 and find the folded components. Then do it
   again at 4× oversampling and compare.
6. **Fixed-point cubic.** Implement the soft clip in Q15 with a Q16 reciprocal of 3, and check
   its monotonicity and bound over all int16 inputs.

## Build

Start from module 10 or later. Distortion only needs the mixer and the patch table.

**Add:**

- **Four flavors** plus "none", selected per patch. *Example* signatures (illustrative):

  ```cpp
  enum Dist { NONE, HARD, SOFT, FOLD, CRUSH };
  int16_t distort(int16_t x, Dist d);  // integer only, bounded time
  ```
- **The encoder button** toggles the active patch's distortion. *Example:* `dist: SOFT on`.
- **Concrete shapes.** *Examples:*
  - hard: `clamp(x · 6)`
  - soft: `x' = clamp(x · 6)`, then `x' − x'³/3` in Q15
  - fold: reflect at ±12,000 (at most 8 reflections, then clamp)
  - crush: zero the low 8 bits
- **A placement choice**, per-voice or post-mix, stated in one place in the code. *Example:*
  `#define DIST_STAGE_PRE_MIX`, with a compile error if both or neither are defined.
- **A cost check.** *Example:* `audio: 32 voices + pre-mix SOFT = 920 cyc/frame (25%)`.

**Constraints:**

- Integer math only and no divides on the per-sample path.
- Bounded time: no loop whose iteration count depends on the input without a cap.
- When distortion is off, the shaper isn't called at all (no "NONE" branch per sample).

**Deliverable:** a distortion unit, a per-patch flavor field, and the toggle.

## Design questions

1. Per voice or post-mix? Weigh the cycle cost (×N), the sound (intermodulation), and what
   "drive" means as the voice count changes.
2. Where does distortion sit relative to the envelope and the filter? (Think of a guitar amp:
   which of these is "before" the amp?)
3. Soft clip wants `/3`. Why is a reciprocal multiply better here than a shift by a power of
   two?
4. Should distortion be a fixed property of a patch, or should the toggle let any patch use
   any flavor?

## Compare (spoiler)

- `synth/distortion.h`: `DistortionType`, the `DISTORTION_STAGE_PRE_MIX`/`POST_MIX` choice
  with its `#error` guard, `DISTORTION_DRIVE_GAIN` 6, `DISTORTION_FOLD_THRESHOLD` 12000 and
  `DISTORTION_BITCRUSH_BITS` 8.
- `synth/distortion.cpp`:
  - `softClip()` uses `kCubicReciprocalQ16` = 21846 instead of `/ 3`. The comment explains
    why a power-of-two divisor would break the curve's monotonicity.
  - `foldback()` caps reflections at `kMaxFoldIterations` = 8 and then clamps.
  - `bitcrush()` is a single mask.
- `synth/audio_task.cpp`: distortion is applied per voice inside `#if
  defined(DISTORTION_STAGE_PRE_MIX)` and only `if (distortionEnabled)`, so it's skipped
  entirely when off.
- `synth/voices.h`: `Patch::distortion`, the "matched distortion" per patch (e.g. METL1 hard
  clip, METL2 foldback).
- `synth/test/test_softclip.cpp`: the host test for the cubic's reciprocal.
