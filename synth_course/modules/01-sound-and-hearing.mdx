## Goal

Build a working model of what a musical sound physically is and how the ear reads it, so every
later design choice has something to aim at.

## You'll be able to

- Describe a periodic sound as a fundamental plus harmonics, and predict its spectrum.
- Use dB for amplitude ratios and cents for pitch ratios fluently.
- Explain why pitch is heard logarithmically and loudness roughly logarithmically.
- Distinguish what the ear is sensitive to (the spectrum and how it changes) from what it
  mostly ignores (static phase relationships).

## Concepts

- **Waves.** Pressure, frequency, period, amplitude, and simple harmonic motion.
- **The harmonic series**, and why a vibrating string or air column produces one.
- **Timbre** as a spectral envelope *plus* how it changes in time (attack, decay and vibrato
  come later in the course).
- **Decibels.** `20·log10(a2/a1)` for amplitudes: +6 dB is about ×2, +20 dB is ×10.
- **Pitch perception.** Equal *ratios* sound like equal steps. Octaves, semitones and cents
  (1/1200 of an octave). The missing fundamental.
- **Loudness perception.** Roughly logarithmic, and frequency-dependent (equal-loudness
  contours).
- **Phase.** For steady tones the ear is largely phase-deaf. Hold on to that for module 07.

## Explore (notebook)

`01-sound-and-hearing.ipynb`:

1. Synthesize 1 s of a sine at 220 Hz, then sums of harmonics with 1/n, odd-only 1/n and odd
   1/n² amplitudes (saw, square and triangle). Plot two periods of each, plus a log-magnitude
   spectrum.
2. **Phase test.** Render the saw with random harmonic phases. Plot it (it looks nothing like
   a saw) and listen to it A/B against the original.
3. **Missing fundamental.** Render harmonics 2–8 of 220 Hz with no 220 Hz component. Compare
   its pitch to a 220 Hz sine.
4. **dB drill.** Write `db(a)` and `amp(db)` and tabulate the saw's harmonic levels in dB.
5. **Write the reference set** to WAV (16-bit, 44.1 kHz): sine, saw, square, triangle at
   220 Hz, plus an A minor triad of sines. Later modules compare your hardware's recorded
   output against these.

**Aha:** "timbre" is mostly *which harmonics, how loud*. The waveform's shape is a poor guide.

## Play (playground)

**Harmonic ear trainer.**
- 16 harmonic toggles with level sliders and a fundamental slider.
- A "drop fundamental" switch and a "scramble phases" button.
- A live waveform and spectrum.
- You should hear the pitch stay put when the fundamental drops out, and the waveform change
  wildly under "scramble phases" while the sound barely does.

**No firmware this week.** You build the reference material the hardware will be judged

**Add:**

- **A reference WAV set** in a `reference/` folder. *Example* file names: `sine_220.wav`,
  `saw_220.wav`, `square_220.wav`, `triangle_220.wav`, `am_triad.wav`.
- **A one-page "ear sheet"** of your own notes: the formulas you'll reuse (Hz ↔ MIDI, cents,
  dB). *Example* lines:
  - `cents = 1200·log2(f2/f1)`
  - `MIDI = 69 + 12·log2(f/440)`
  - `dB = 20·log10(a2/a1)`

**Deliverable:** the reference WAVs plus the ear sheet.

## Design questions

1. If phase barely matters for steady tones, what does that buy you when you build waveforms
   to fit in a 16-bit range (module 07)?
2. Pitch is logarithmic. What does that suggest about how a pitch knob should map rotation to
   Hz (module 05)?
3. Loudness is logarithmic too. Should a volume knob be linear in amplitude? Note what you'd
   guess now and check it in module 05.
