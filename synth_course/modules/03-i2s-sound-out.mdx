## Goal

Get audio samples out of the chip, as a clean, correctly pitched tone through both the line-out
DAC and the speaker amp.

## You'll be able to

- Explain the I2S frame: bit clock, word select (LR clock) and data, plus the one-bit delay.
- Configure the ESP32-C3's I2S peripheral for 44.1 kHz, 16-bit stereo.
- Wire two I2S receivers in parallel on the same three lines.
- Produce a sine from a lookup table and feed the peripheral continuously.

## Concepts

- **I2S signals.** BCK (bit clock), LRCK/WS (word select), DIN/SD (data) and, optionally, MCLK.
  At 44.1 kHz × 16 bits × 2 channels, BCK = 1.4112 MHz.
- **Frame format.** MSB first, data one BCK after the WS edge (Philips standard), and left
  while WS is low.
- **The PCM5102A.** Its internal PLL lets it run without a master clock (tie SCK to GND).
  Hardware-strapped options: FLT (filter), DEMP (de-emphasis), FMT (format), XSMT (soft
  mute). It gives line-level out.
- **The MAX98357A.** A class-D amp with I2S in, mono (it mixes L+R by default), fixed gain and
  a speaker out.
- **DMA and blocking writes.** The driver owns ring buffers. When they're full, `write()`
  blocks, so *the DAC's clock paces your code*. That's the heartbeat of everything from
  module 04 on.
- **A sine from a table.** Compute `sin()` once into an `int16_t` table, then step through it.

## Build

Start from module 00's sketch, still with no tasks (everything in `setup()`/`loop()`).

**Add:**

- **I2S output** at 44.1 kHz, 16-bit stereo, on your chosen three pins, wired to *both* the DAC
  and the amp. *Example* wiring note for your pin header:

  ```
  BCK  -> PCM5102 BCK,  MAX98357 BCLK
  LRCK -> PCM5102 LCK,  MAX98357 LRC
  DOUT -> PCM5102 DIN,  MAX98357 DIN
  PCM5102: SCK->GND, FLT/DEMP/FMT = L, XSMT = H
  ```
- **A sine table** built at boot and stepped through at 440 Hz. *Example:* a 1,024-entry table
  at amplitude 32,000. The step per sample for 440 Hz is `1024 × 440 / 44100 ≈ 10.22`
  entries. How you handle the fraction is up to you (module 06 does it properly).
- **Continuous feeding.** Fill a block of interleaved stereo frames and write it, forever.
  *Example:* 256 frames = 1,024 bytes = 5.8 ms of audio per write.
- **A boot line.** *Example:* `i2s: 44100 Hz, 16-bit stereo, 256-frame blocks`.

**Constraints:**

- The output never stops: any gap is an audible click.
- The level stays below full scale, so leave yourself a little headroom.
- No `Serial` prints inside the feeding loop (try one to hear what it does, then remove it).

**Deliverable:** a sketch that boots straight into a clean tone on both outputs.

## Design questions

1. What block size will you write per call? Trade latency (control response) against
   per-call overhead.
2. The MAX98357A sums L+R and the DAC plays them separately. If you ever want true stereo,
   what changes? For now, should L and R carry the same sample?
3. You need a fractional step through the table. Float phase, fixed-point phase, or integer
   steps with a slightly wrong pitch? (Module 06 settles this, so note your instinct now.)
4. Why does a blocking `write()` make `delay()`-based timing unnecessary?
